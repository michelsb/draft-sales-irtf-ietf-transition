     A Method for Tracing IRTF Research Contributions to IETF Work
                draft-sales-irtf-ietf-transition-latest

Abstract

   This document describes a reproducible method for investigating
   whether and how Internet Research Task Force (IRTF) research
   contributes to Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) work.  The
   method combines a versioned study plan, preservation of source
   material, typed discovery of documentary, organizational, and human
   mediation hypotheses, and independent human review of located
   evidence.  It distinguishes discovery signals from confirmed
   contributions and defines separate classifications for transfer
   mechanisms, transferred objects, and evidence observations.  It also
   describes coverage audits, reporting requirements, and limits on
   interpreting citations, document continuity, group relationships,
   shared authorship, and event dates.  The accompanying reference
   implementation supports collection, discovery, review, and
   descriptive reporting; it does not automatically establish research
   use or causality.

1.  Introduction

   The IRTF organizes research on Internet-related topics, while the
   IETF develops engineering specifications and standards.  Their
   different roles are described in [RFC2014].  Research can contribute
   to IETF work through document continuity, use of a technical
   solution, adoption of a concept or experimental result,
   organizational processes, or mediation by participants.  These
   mechanisms need not coincide with publication of a document or
   creation of a working group.

   The research question addressed by this method is:

      Which IRTF results were demonstrably used in IETF work, through
      which mechanisms, and with what observed reach?

   Answering this question requires more than counting references or
   matching draft names.  An RFC number identifies a publication, not an
   IETF contribution.  The IRTF has its own document stream [RFC5743],
   and publication streams and standards status are distinct [RFC7841].
   Similarly, working-group adoption without a verified IRTF origin does
   not establish a research contribution.

   This document provides an operational method for constructing and
   reviewing contribution hypotheses.  Its main design principle is to
   preserve a trace from each conclusion to the source version, passage,
   chronology, and review decision supporting it.  Automated discovery
   identifies material for inspection; human review establishes whether
   the mechanism-specific conditions are met.

1.1.  Scope and Status

   This is an individual methodological proposal.  It does not claim
   research-group adoption, IRTF consensus, or IRSG approval.  The
   intended submission stream in the source metadata does not establish
   any of those conditions.

   The method covers documentary, organizational, and human mediation
   inquiries using recoverable historical sources.  The reference
   implementation automatically collects selected Datatracker and RFC
   Editor sources.  Complementary process records can be needed to
   confirm a case even when they are outside that automated collection
   coverage.

   This document describes a method and its implementation boundaries.
   It reports no completed corpus, confirmed transfers, coverage
   estimate, or measured reviewer agreement.  Each empirical application
   needs its own registered study plan and results.

2.  Terminology and Units of Analysis

   The words "requires" and "confirmation condition" below describe the
   research procedure.  This document does not define a network protocol
   or use BCP 14 requirement keywords.

   IRTF research group (RG):  A research group whose eligibility and
      historical identity are established in the study inventory.

   IETF working group (WG):  A working group relevant to an observed
      IETF destination or process.  A current affiliation is not
      automatically a historical affiliation.

   Origin and destination:  The research-side and IETF-side endpoints of
      a proposed contribution.  An endpoint can be a document or a
      group, depending on the inquiry.

   Candidate:  A hypothesis selected for review.  Documentary candidates
      concern document pairs, organizational candidates concern RG/WG
      processes, and human mediation candidates concern an observed
      person/address hint and its possible connection to a contribution.

   Discovery signal:  A recorded reason for proposing a candidate.
      Several signals and paths can support the same candidate.  Signals
      are not findings.

   Evidence observation:  A located observation in an identified source
      version, with provenance, integrity information, date information,
      and a stated relationship to the hypothesis.

   Review:  A person's justified decision about a candidate and its
      evidence.  Corrections retain the preceding decision in the review
      history.

   Consolidated decision:  Agreement between independent review roles,
      or an adjudicated decision under the study plan.  This is distinct
      from consolidating several document pairs into one scientific
      event.

   Confirmed contribution event:  A contribution meeting the applicable
      confirmation conditions after review.  A single event can carry
      multiple mechanism and object labels.  Related candidates need an
      explicit event-consolidation decision before they can be counted
      as one event.

   Run:  An execution associated with a frozen study-plan version,
      historical cutoff, inventory, and reviewer assignments.  Sources
      and derived snapshots can accumulate within that fixed scope.

   An address is not a verified person; a document pair is not a
   confirmed event; and a signal count is not a count of contributions.

3.  Study Design and Eligibility

3.1.  Versioned Study Plan

   Before collection, register the following study choices:

   1.  The research question, inclusion and exclusion criteria, and
       intended interpretation of a contribution.

   2.  An upper historical cutoff, represented by the last instant of a
       selected month in UTC.

   3.  A verified inventory of active and concluded RGs, including
       acronyms and persistent numeric Datatracker identifiers, with a
       documented inventory audit.

   4.  The publication plan for results, evidence, and limitations.

   5.  Separate first-reviewer, second-reviewer, and adjudicator roles,
       or explicit limitations where those roles cannot be assigned.

   6.  Procedures for coverage auditing, independent review,
       disagreement resolution, and assessment of agreement before
       adjudication.

   7.  The selection design if only part of the eligible universe will
       receive manual inspection.

   Freeze these choices when creating a run.  The reference documentary
   profile considers all recoverable history up to the cutoff, drafts
   and RFCs in all states, and recoverable IETF documentary
   destinations.  The cutoff is an upper bound, not the beginning of the
   search period.

   A bounded HTTP request budget or indexing batch is an execution
   control, not a scientific sample.  If the question, eligibility
   scope, or scientific confirmation criteria change, register a new
   plan version and run.  Applying an updated discovery rule to
   preserved sources can enlarge a queue within the existing scope,
   provided the rule version, input snapshot, and earlier decisions
   remain auditable.

3.2.  Endpoint Eligibility

   Establish an origin's IRTF affiliation from preserved group,
   document, or historical records, checked against the frozen
   inventory.  A name prefix alone is insufficient.  Establish the
   destination's IETF affiliation independently; publication as an RFC
   is insufficient.

   Retain ambiguous, unknown, and multiple affiliations explicitly.  An
   unambiguous current label does not resolve a conflicting history.
   Where a single documentary identity appears in both institutional
   phases, review the ordered phase observations rather than treating
   the two labels as two independent documents.

   Known post-cutoff endpoints and intermediates are excluded from
   eligible historical paths.  Undated material remains temporally
   pending where its eligibility cannot be established.  Collection time
   does not substitute for an event date.

4.  Sources, Preservation, and Chronology

4.1.  Source Coverage

   The automated documentary sources are the Datatracker API
   [DATATRACKER-API] and RFC Editor material, including the RFC index
   [RFC-INDEX] and preserved document texts.

    +===========================+====================================+
    | Source material           | Research purpose                   |
    +===========================+====================================+
    | Document metadata,        | Identity, affiliation, continuity, |
    | events, and relationships | and citation observations          |
    +---------------------------+------------------------------------+
    | RFC index and draft/RFC   | Publication metadata, references,  |
    | texts                     | and passages for content review    |
    +---------------------------+------------------------------------+
    | Group metadata, history,  | RG/WG screening, institutional     |
    | and selected charters     | observations, and agenda clues     |
    +---------------------------+------------------------------------+
    | Posted draft submissions  | Dated author-address observations  |
    +---------------------------+------------------------------------+

                                 Table 1

   Meeting minutes, BOF records, slides, mailing-list discussions, code,
   and development records can provide complementary evidence.  The
   implemented discovery workflow does not comprehensively collect or
   analyze them, complete participation histories, or person aliases.
   Record which of these sources were actually searched in each study.

   Completing the pagination of a query establishes completion of that
   query, not complete institutional history.  Missing historical texts,
   unavailable revisions, inconsistent pagination, and failed requests
   remain visible acquisition gaps.

4.2.  Preservation and Provenance

   Preserve original response and file bytes separately from normalized
   records and derived candidates.  Associate artifacts with source
   URLs, request parameters where applicable, collection times, source
   versions, and SHA-256 digests.  Keep request logs and resumable
   checkpoints so that an interrupted acquisition can be distinguished
   from an empty result.

   Derived observations retain their source references, transformation
   or rule identifiers, and analytical snapshot.  Reanalysis preserves
   candidate identifiers, investigator annotations, and review history.
   A lead not proposed by a newer snapshot can remain as a historical
   candidate; report that condition rather than silently deleting it.

   A checksum verifies equality with the preserved bytes.  It does not
   prove that the source statement is true, historically complete, or
   independent of another source.  Copies of the same declaration do not
   constitute independent corroboration.

4.3.  Distinct Dates

   Keep source-event time, publication time, observation time, and
   collection time separate.  Preserve the precision supplied by the
   source, including year-only or month-only dates and unknown dates.

   Charter revision observation, revision publication, and charter
   approval are distinct observations.  The implementation can use
   preserved Datatracker new_revision and iesg_approved events to
   support the latter dates.  Binding an approval recorded under a draft
   revision to the next numbered approved revision requires a matching
   publication timestamp; missing or ambiguous events leave the binding
   pending.

   Even a supported charter approval date does not establish when the
   originating research became available or when its adoption occurred.
   Those are separate chronological questions for review.

5.  Normalization and Typed Documentary Paths

   Normalize document identifiers, explicit aliases, inverse
   relationship forms, and definite historical renames while retaining
   the original records.  Keep documentary succession separate from
   technical evolution, series membership, publication stream, and
   standards status.

   Preserve both the factual relationship direction and the projected
   direction of a possible research contribution.  For example, a source
   record can point from a citing document to a cited document, while
   the contribution hypothesis is evaluated from cited work to citing
   work.

   The relationship vocabulary is taken from the preserved Datatracker
   catalog [RELATIONSHIP-CATALOG].  The following families are local
   screening categories, not an IETF taxonomy of scientific transfer.

    +===============+===============================+================+
    | Family        | Inputs                        | Contribution   |
    |               |                               | projection     |
    +===============+===============================+================+
    | CIT: citation | refnorm, refinfo, refold,     | Cited to       |
    |               | parsed references; possible   | citing work    |
    |               | refunk                        |                |
    +---------------+-------------------------------+----------------+
    | CONT:         | replaces, became_rfc,         | Earlier work   |
    | continuity    | definite historical renames;  | to successor   |
    |               | possible possibly-replaces    |                |
    +---------------+-------------------------------+----------------+
    | EVOL:         | updates, obs, and normalized  | Updated or     |
    | technical     | inverse forms                 | obsoleted work |
    | evolution     |                               | to later work  |
    +---------------+-------------------------------+----------------+
    | FASE:         | Explicit stream-change events | IRTF phase     |
    | institutional | or ordered affiliation        | before IETF    |
    | phase         | observations of one identity  | phase          |
    |               | or definite lineage           |                |
    +---------------+-------------------------------+----------------+

                                 Table 2

   Normative and informative references serve different purposes
   [RFC3967].  Preserve that distinction without treating either subtype
   as automatic proof that a particular research object was used.

5.1.  Admissible Paths

   Let C denote a definite succession connection, B a cited-to-citing
   connection, E an update/obsolescence connection from earlier to later
   work, and M membership from an RFC to an STD or BCP series.  The
   symbol * permits zero or more connections; + requires at least one.

           +=============+====================================+
           | Family      | Admissible pattern                 |
           +=============+====================================+
           | CONT        | C+                                 |
           +-------------+------------------------------------+
           | CIT         | C* B C*, containing exactly one    |
           |             | citation                           |
           +-------------+------------------------------------+
           | EVOL        | E+                                 |
           +-------------+------------------------------------+
           | CIT through | M B, pending component and         |
           | a series    | historical-membership review       |
           +-------------+------------------------------------+
           | FASE        | A same-identity event or ordered   |
           |             | observations; alternatively, C+    |
           |             | with ordered endpoint affiliations |
           +-------------+------------------------------------+

                                 Table 3

   Direct means one admissible connection.  Chain means two or more
   connections through intermediate items.  A candidate with both direct
   and chain signals belongs to the "direct and chain" group.  These
   labels describe structure, not confidence or confirmation.

   Figure 2 illustrates these groups using CIT paths.  A is an eligible
   IRTF origin, D is an eligible IETF destination, and X is an
   intermediate document.  B and C retain their definitions above.  The
   examples assume that the displayed paths are the recorded support for
   each pair.

   (a) Direct only
       A --------B--------> D

   (b) Chain only
       A ---C---> X ---B---> D

   (c) Direct and chain: one candidate, two paths
       A --------B--------> D
       A ---C---> X ---B---> D

   (d) Excluded as a CIT path: two successive citations
       A ---B---> X ---B---> D

          Figure 2: Illustrative CIT paths and candidate topology

   Panel (c) has the same endpoints on both lines and is counted as one
   candidate.  Topology groups are mutually exclusive at candidate
   level; individual discovery families and signals can overlap.  Panel
   (d) does not establish use of A by D and cannot serve as a CIT path.

   Possible references and replacements create direct leads only; they
   do not serve as transitive bridges.  Two successive citations do not
   establish use of the earliest work.  Shared ancestors, undirected
   connectivity, and shared series membership do not establish
   succession.  The engine selects representative admissible paths, not
   every path or a generic transitive closure of the graph.

   An RFC-to-series membership from contains or RFC-index is-also can
   resolve a series citation into a pending component hypothesis.
   Present-day membership does not establish historical composition or
   which component the citing document used.

   The relationships downref-approval, conflrev, and the status-process
   codes tops, tois, tobcp, toexp, toinf, and tohist supply context.
   They are not independent technical-transfer signals or generic
   bridges.  Approval, conflict review, and standards-status changes do
   not by themselves establish a stream transition or use.

   For FASE, affiliation-derived ordering uses the earliest known IETF
   presence; an explicit event supports the local transition it records.
   Neither general connectivity nor endpoint ordering dates every link.

6.  Candidate Discovery

   The three categories below identify different units of inspection.
   One underlying case can generate related candidates in several
   categories.  The categories do not compete as alternative final
   labels.  Manual hypotheses can also be recorded, without inventing
   automated discovery signals.

6.1.  Documentary Candidates

   Documentary discovery proposes eligible origin/destination document
   pairs using CIT, CONT, EVOL, and FASE.  It retains the supporting
   paths, relationship subtypes, factual and projected directions,
   source locations, and unresolved chronology or affiliation checks.

   Review determines whether an identifiable research object was used,
   whether documentary continuity is established, or whether the
   relationship is only contextual.  A citation is a discovery
   observation, not an automatic T2, T3, or T4 finding.

6.2.  Organizational Candidates

   Organizational discovery investigates an RG/WG pair and a possible
   process linking research to the creation or change of IETF activity.
   The following are derived rules, not additional Datatracker document
   relationship codes:

    +=========================+======================================+
    | Signal                  | Screening observation                |
    +=========================+======================================+
    | ORG_DOCUMENT_LINKS      | A documentary candidate connects an  |
    |                         | observed RG document and WG document |
    +-------------------------+--------------------------------------+
    | ORG_DOCUMENT_CONTINUITY | That documentary support also        |
    |                         | carries continuity evidence          |
    +-------------------------+--------------------------------------+
    | ORG_CHARTER_REFERENCE   | A verified preserved WG charter      |
    |                         | contains a located RG mention        |
    +-------------------------+--------------------------------------+
    | ORG_TYPE_CHANGE         | Verified consecutive dated           |
    |                         | observations show RG then WG for the |
    |                         | same persistent group identifier     |
    +-------------------------+--------------------------------------+
    | ORG_AGENDA_ADOPTION     | A preserved charter contains         |
    |                         | explicit RG research attribution and |
    |                         | a nearby WG work mandate             |
    +-------------------------+--------------------------------------+

                                 Table 4

   Document-supported group signals have chain topology.  Verified
   charter mentions, same-identifier type changes, and agenda-adoption
   signals have direct topology.  These structures do not establish
   group succession or research adoption.

   The origin belongs to the frozen RG inventory.  Supporting
   documentary candidates and artifacts belong to the same run.
   Charter-based rules enrich the saved group-screening universe; they
   are not an exhaustive independent search across all charters.
   Different-identifier group succession and BOF or other process routes
   can remain undiscovered.

   The agenda-adoption rule uses narrow English patterns for research
   developed or defined by an RG, together with a nearby mandate to
   update, integrate, adopt, standardize, or add work to protocols.  The
   two sentence starts are within 1,600 characters.  The rule preserves
   the attribution and mandate passages separately and proposes a
   possible signal on the existing RG/WG candidate.

   Review checks that both passages concern the same research result,
   that the origin predates the relevant mandate, and that process
   evidence supports the claimed connection.  Textual proximity, a group
   mention, cooperation, or thematic similarity is insufficient.
   Adoption of work is distinct from succession of organizations.

6.3.  Human Mediation Candidates

   Human discovery uses usable author addresses in preserved, posted
   draft submissions within the cutoff.  Domain normalization preserves
   local-part case.  Matching is by exact resulting address, not by name
   similarity or inferred aliases.  RFC authorship is not automatically
   inherited from predecessor drafts.

6.3.1.  Documentary-Base Shared Authorship

   HUM_SHARED_AUTHOR compares authorship observations on the exact
   endpoint document names of an existing documentary candidate.  An
   address observed on both sides creates a possible chain through that
   address hint, linked to the documentary base.  A single author list
   is not duplicated into two institutional phases of the same document.

   The base can still be pending when discovery occurs.  This route does
   not apply the independent route's stricter historical-phase and
   submission-order screening.  Its output therefore has a different
   screening scope.

6.3.2.  Independent Author Intersections

   HUM_AUTHOR_INTERSECTION searches preserved submissions without
   requiring an existing documentary candidate.  Each usable authorship
   observation requires an unambiguous historical phase observed by the
   start of its submission day.  The origin submission is strictly
   earlier than the destination submission.  Same-document pairs and
   conflicting or unknown phases are excluded.

   Pairs with an existing documentary candidate or a recorded direct
   relationship in either direction are separated from this exploratory
   queue.  Remaining address matches create human mediation leads
   without a linked base.  The absence of a recorded direct relationship
   does not exclude indirect paths or missing evidence.  Indirect paths
   are not exhaustively searched by this screening step.

   One lead represents one address/document-pair observation, not one
   verified person or event.  Identity, historical group types,
   continuous affiliations, actual contribution, and mediation remain
   unverified.  This route is a separate discovery action; completion of
   the documentary-base route does not imply completion of this search.

6.3.3.  Linking and Confirming a Mediation Claim

   A human lead can later be associated with a documentary or
   organizational base in the same run, with a recorded justification.
   That association does not confirm the contribution or the person.  T6
   confirmation requires an independently consolidated T1--T5 base,
   verified participation, and a located passage documenting mediation.

   Figure 3 shows the path to T6 confirmation.  An address match can
   generate a lead but cannot satisfy the identity or role conditions by
   itself.

     Lead without a base             Lead with a base
              |                              |
              +---------------+--------------+
                              v
                 Link / check the base event
                              |
                              v
       +---------------------------------------------+
       | All T6 confirmation conditions are required |
       |                                             |
       | Independently confirmed T1-T5 base event     |
       | Verified identity and participation         |
       | Located evidence of a mediation role        |
       +---------------------------------------------+
                              |
                              v
                    Independent T6 review
                              |
                       if confirmed
                              v
                T6 attached to the existing event

          Figure 3: Confirmation dependencies for human mediation

   The figure shows confirmation dependencies, not the order of all
   review activity.  Uncertainty or rejection can be recorded while the
   base is unresolved.  A confirmed mediation finding adds a mechanism
   to the existing event; it does not create another base event.

   Changing the base retains history but prevents confirmation about the
   former base from being applied to the new association.  If the base
   loses confirmation, dependent mediation becomes pending without
   deleting its reviews.

7.  Classification and Confirmation Conditions

   The following codes form the project's analytical taxonomy.
   Discovery families, mechanism labels, object labels, and evidence
   codes describe different things and are recorded separately.

7.1.  Transfer Mechanisms

       +======+================+==================================+
       | Code | Mechanism      | Minimum confirmation condition   |
       +======+================+==================================+
       | T1   | Documentary    | Verified IRTF-to-IETF continuity |
       |      |                | supported by history or an       |
       |      |                | explicit statement, checked      |
       |      |                | against content and affiliations |
       +------+----------------+----------------------------------+
       | T2   | Technical      | A located passage demonstrates   |
       |      |                | use of an identifiable origin    |
       |      |                | solution                         |
       +------+----------------+----------------------------------+
       | T3   | Conceptual     | Explicit attribution and use of  |
       |      |                | an origin concept, model, or     |
       |      |                | requirement                      |
       +------+----------------+----------------------------------+
       | T4   | Experimental   | An identifiable experimental     |
       |      | or empirical   | result is used in a destination  |
       |      |                | decision or specification        |
       +------+----------------+----------------------------------+
       | T5   | Organizational | A process record links a         |
       |      | or process     | research agenda to creation or   |
       |      |                | change of IETF activity          |
       +------+----------------+----------------------------------+
       | T6   | Human-mediated | Verified participation on both   |
       |      |                | sides and a documented mediation |
       |      |                | role in an independently         |
       |      |                | confirmed T1--T5 event           |
       +------+----------------+----------------------------------+

                                 Table 5

   For T1, validate each claimed rename, adoption, publication-
   continuity, or institutional-phase path.  WG adoption alone is
   insufficient.  For T2, normative dependence identifies the content
   actually used; a normative reference alone is insufficient.  Similar
   terminology or a background citation is insufficient for T3.  Citing
   an experiment without demonstrated use is insufficient for T4.
   Thematic proximity between groups is insufficient for T5.
   Authorship, chair status, or participation alone is insufficient for
   T6.

   T6 is auxiliary: it describes mediation of an established event and
   cannot establish the underlying contribution by itself.  Mechanisms
   are not mutually exclusive.  Adding mechanism labels does not
   multiply the number of consolidated events.

8.  Independent Review and Decision History

   Assign first and second reviews to different people and adjudication
   to a third person.  Separate accounts support the workflow but do not
   by themselves establish independent judgment.

   Use the following review sequence:

   1.  Inspect the candidate unit, discovery reasons, preserved paths,
       source coverage, and unresolved checks.

   2.  Verify endpoint identity, historical affiliation, chronology, and
       the identifiable object or agenda under investigation.

   3.  Locate the relevant passage in its preserved revision and inspect
       the surrounding context, provenance, and counterevidence.

   4.  Apply the T1--T6 conditions and O1--O16 labels.  Record a
       confirmed, rejected, or uncertain decision and its justification.

   5.  Have the second reviewer decide independently before revealing
       either review to the other reviewer.

   6.  Consolidate matching decisions or adjudicate disagreements only
       after both reviews exist.  Preserve the disagreement and
       rationale.

   In the reference application, agreement requires matching decision
   state, mechanism labels, and object labels.  A separate adjudicator
   can resolve a disagreement after both reviews.  Pending means that no
   effective consolidated decision exists; unresolved disagreement
   remains distinguishable from uncertainty and rejection.

   Figure 4 summarizes the decision flow.  Reviewers complete their own
   decisions before comparison.  Agreement is assessed before
   adjudication; both agreement and adjudication can result in a
   confirmed, rejected, or uncertain consolidated decision.

   Missing reviews and disagreement awaiting adjudication remain
   pending.  All branches retain the individual decisions and any later
   corrections.

   One positive review does not establish independent confirmation.  If
   the study lacks a second reviewer or adjudicator, disclose the
   resulting limitation instead of reporting that role as completed.
   Measure agreement before adjudication as described in Section 9.

   Corrections append a new review linked to the preceding decision.
   Annotations do not silently rewrite scientific judgments.  New source
   material or a changed base association can require an explicit new
   review.  Retain all versions, while identifying which decisions are
   currently effective.

   For mediation, also enforce the base and role-evidence conditions in
   Section 6.3.  A source code indicating authorship or chair
   participation does not satisfy the mediation-role requirement by
   itself.  Software checks can enforce required fields and
   dependencies; they cannot determine that a quoted passage actually
   proves the claimed role.

9.  Validation and Coverage Auditing

   Audit the eligible origin universe independently of the discovered
   queue.  Reviewing only retrieved candidates can assess those
   candidates but cannot estimate cases missed by discovery.

   If using a probability audit, declare its sampling frame, strata,
   sample sizes, random seed, inclusion probabilities, and precision
   targets.  Inspect selected origins using complementary searches and
   record which eligible connections were absent from discovery.  Report
   difficult or illustrative cases selected purposively separately from
   the probability sample.  The reference application does not generate
   or evaluate such a sample automatically.

   Assess independent reviewer agreement before adjudication.
   Percentage agreement and Cohen's kappa can be reported, accompanied
   by label prevalence, the reviewed units, missing decisions, and the
   treatment of multiple labels.  For multilabel coding, state whether
   agreement is measured per label, for exact label sets, or both.
   Preserve the original disagreements even after adjudication.

   Document coding-manual revisions and distinguish pilot calibration
   from independent evaluation.  Source-coverage audits, implementation
   tests, and scientific validation answer different questions.  Passing
   software tests does not validate the completeness of the corpus, the
   interpretation of a passage, or a scientific conclusion.

10.  Analysis and Reporting

10.1.  Counts and Consolidation

   Report separately:

   *  Eligible origin units and audited coverage gaps.

   *  Unique candidates by category and discovery snapshot.

   *  Discovery families and signals, allowing for overlap.

   *  Direct-only, chain-only, direct-and-chain, and unknown-topology
      candidate groups, each candidate appearing once in this partition.

   *  Individual and consolidated review states, including unresolved
      disagreements and missing reviews.

   *  Confirmed findings and any manually consolidated contribution
      events, with their mechanism and object labels.

   Before reporting event totals, state the rule for deciding whether
   different document versions, paths, group hypotheses, or mediation
   claims concern the same contribution.  Maintain their mappings to the
   consolidated event.  The implementation's candidate deduplication and
   review consolidation do not automatically provide this scientific
   event consolidation.

   A transfer rate needs a validated denominator of eligible,
   consolidated origin units.  State whether the numerator counts
   origins with at least one event or counts events, and ensure the
   units match the intended measure.  Candidate counts and citation
   counts are not substitutes for that numerator.  Label totals can
   exceed event totals because mechanisms and objects are multilabel.

10.2.  Dates and Observed Reach

   Publication-year charts and approximate destination dates describe
   the observed documentary material.  They are not validated transfer
   dates or transfer-latency estimates.  Identify the date basis,
   precision, and provenance of each reported series, including fallback
   approximations.

   Origins without an observed event by the cutoff are right-censored,
   not assigned a fabricated event date or assumed never to contribute.
   Unequal follow-up periods, incomplete histories, and different
   collection coverage can change observed rates and timelines.

   Observed reach can be described through confirmed destination
   documents, objects, or groups where the study has validated those
   units and affiliations.  Do not interpret raw citation counts as
   technical use or research quality.  Descriptive association does not
   establish that IRTF participation caused a destination outcome.

10.3.  Reproducibility Package

   Publish, subject to the evidence-release plan and privacy decisions:

   1.  The frozen study plan, inventory audit, and coding-manual
       version.

   2.  Collection scope, requests, checkpoints, unavailable sources, and
       integrity manifests for preserved artifacts.

   3.  Normalization and discovery-rule versions, analytical input
       snapshots, and source-to-candidate provenance.

   4.  Evidence observations, review histories, adjudication rationales,
       effective decisions, and event-consolidation mappings.

   5.  Analysis definitions, filters, date bases, and the scripts or
       procedures needed to reproduce reported counts and tables.

   6.  Coverage-audit and agreement results, or an explicit statement
       that these validation steps have not been completed.

   Distinguish the historical input of a candidate from newer source
   collections and screening reports.  Historical snapshot totals and
   current run-wide totals can differ because older candidates are
   retained and new evidence has been added.

11.  Reference Implementation and Limitations

   The accompanying research kit supports preserved source collection,
   incremental normalization, typed documentary discovery,
   organizational and human screening, source inspection, independent
   reviews, append-only corrections, descriptive charts, and exports.  A
   file-based Python engine supports documentary execution; the web
   application adds study management and the organizational and human
   mediation workflows.

   Discovery on saved inputs is separate from source acquisition.
   Record the execution and coverage status of each route; indexing in
   progress, missing inputs, unavailable sources, and zero matches are
   different outcomes.  Updating the software does not itself rerun
   discovery.

   The implementation does not automatically verify technical,
   conceptual, or empirical use, resolve global person identity,
   consolidate every candidate into scientific events, construct a
   probability audit, calculate reviewer agreement, or provide causal or
   social-network analysis.  Those activities require a declared design
   and additional work.

   Coverage is limited by mutable metadata, incomplete archives, missing
   group histories, unavailable revisions, ambiguous identities, partial
   charter screening, narrow English lexical rules, and undocumented
   influence.  A zero candidate count is not evidence that no
   contribution occurred.  A NO_EVIDENCE conclusion requires documented
   search coverage and is not assigned automatically.

12.  Security Considerations

   This method does not change a network protocol.  Its principal
   security concerns are the integrity of research artifacts, safe
   handling of untrusted source material, and protection of review
   records.  Treat downloaded texts, API fields, and links as untrusted
   input.  Rendering evidence should not execute embedded content, and
   access to collection, review, correction, and export functions should
   follow assigned roles.

   Verify preserved bytes against their recorded hashes when deriving or
   exporting evidence.  Retain acquisition and correction history to
   make unexpected changes detectable.  A matching hash is not
   authentication of a historical claim.  Exporting first-round reviews
   before both independent decisions exist can compromise the study's
   blinding procedure.

13.  Privacy Considerations

   Public submissions and process records can contain names, addresses,
   affiliations, and statements about individuals.  Address matches
   remain identity hints until verified; they do not justify assertions
   about a person's influence, intent, or responsibility.

   The study plan identifies what personal information is needed and
   what can be released.  Publish only the identifying detail needed to
   support the research claim.  Separate reviewer access from public
   release where appropriate, and document any redaction or restricted
   artifact together with its effect on reproducibility.  Preserve
   contradictions and uncertainty rather than turning an automated
   address match into a personal attribution.
