A Method for Tracing IRTF Research Contributions to IETF Work

Abstract

This document describes a reproducible method for investigating whether and how Internet Research Task Force (IRTF) research contributes to Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) work. The method combines a versioned study plan, preservation of source material, typed discovery of documentary, organizational, and human mediation hypotheses, and independent human review of located evidence. It distinguishes discovery signals from confirmed contributions and defines separate classifications for transfer mechanisms, transferred objects, and evidence observations. It also describes coverage audits, reporting requirements, and limits on interpreting citations, document continuity, group relationships, shared authorship, and event dates. Discovery identifies hypotheses for inspection; conclusions about research use require the applicable evidence and independent review.¶

1. Introduction

The IRTF organizes research on Internet-related topics, while the IETF develops engineering specifications and standards. Their different roles are described in [RFC2014]. Research can contribute to IETF work through document continuity, use of a technical solution, adoption of a concept or experimental result, organizational processes, or mediation by participants. These mechanisms need not coincide with publication of a document or creation of a working group.¶

The research question addressed by this method is:¶

  • Which identifiable IRTF research outputs or agendas contributed to IETF work, through which documented mechanisms, and with what observed reach?¶

Answering this question requires more than counting references or matching draft names. An RFC number identifies a publication, not an IETF contribution. The IRTF has its own document stream [RFC5743], and publication streams and standards status are distinct [RFC7841]. Similarly, working-group adoption without a verified IRTF origin does not establish a research contribution.¶

This document provides an operational method for constructing and reviewing contribution hypotheses. Its main design principle is to preserve a trace from each conclusion to the source version, passage, chronology, and review decision supporting it. Automated discovery identifies material for inspection; human review establishes whether the mechanism-specific conditions are met.¶

1.1. Scope and Status

This is an individual methodological proposal. It does not claim research-group adoption, IRTF consensus, or IRSG approval. The intended submission stream in the source metadata does not establish any of those conditions. The proposed classifications and procedures require empirical evaluation of their reliability and coverage as described in Section 10.¶

The method covers documentary, organizational, and human mediation inquiries using recoverable historical sources. Each study declares its source coverage, acquisition procedures, and search limitations. Documentary records and complementary process evidence can both be needed to confirm a case.¶

This document specifies the research procedure and its interpretive limits independently of any particular software or collection system. It reports no completed corpus, confirmed transfers, coverage estimate, or measured reviewer agreement. Each empirical application needs its own registered study plan and results.¶

1.2. Method Overview

Follow the investigation in the sequence below. Each stage produces a record used by the next stage. Record unresolved checks rather than assuming that completion of a stage confirms a contribution.¶

Table 1
Stage Input Actions Output
Plan Research question and eligible universe Declare scope, sources, criteria, reviewers, sampling, and event unit Registered study plan and coding manual
Collect and preserve Declared source scope Retrieve records, retain versions and provenance, document gaps Preserved source collection
Normalize and discover Preserved collection and declared screening rules Reconcile identities, classify relationships, propose hypotheses Candidates with signals, paths, and source references
Inspect and classify Candidate and supporting sources Check eligibility and context, identify the contribution, assess alternatives Evidence record and proposed mechanism and object labels
Review and adjudicate Inspected evidence and criteria Record independent judgments, compare decisions, resolve disagreements Consolidated decision or explicit pending status
Consolidate events Reviewed hypotheses and grouping criteria Establish which records concern the same contribution episode Reviewed event mappings and unresolved groupings
Audit and report Sources, candidate decisions, and event mappings Assess coverage and agreement, distinguish counts and estimates Findings, uncertainty, and reproducibility package

Design the audit before selecting its sample. Source-coverage checks and complementary searches can proceed during the investigation; Section 10 specifies how they reach beyond the candidate queue. A new source or corrected judgment can require revisiting an earlier stage. Retain both the preceding record and the reason for revision. Human mediation leads may start without a base, while T6 confirmation depends on an independently confirmed T1--T5 event.¶

1.3. Four Analytical Layers

Keep the following concepts separate throughout the record:¶

Table 2
Layer Question answered Example
Typed relationship What connection was observed? An IETF draft cites an earlier document
Discovery signal Why inspect this hypothesis? CIT flags an eligible cited-to-citing pair
Mechanism How did the contribution occur? T2 requires evidence of identifiable technical use
Object What was continued or used? O7 identifies an algorithm

A source passage supplies evidence for a claim; the classification records the reviewer's interpretation under the confirmation criteria. The worked example in Section 12 connects these layers to review and event consolidation.¶

2. Terminology and Units of Analysis

The words "requires" and "confirmation condition" below describe the research procedure. This document does not define a network protocol or use BCP 14 requirement keywords.¶

Research contribution:

An identifiable IRTF research output or agenda carried into IETF activity through a mechanism supported under Section 7.2. Documentary continuity (T1), substantive use (T2--T4), and an organizational process (T5) are distinct findings. T6 describes mediation of an established contribution. The transfer labels below refer to these operational classifications.¶

Typed relationship:

A source-supported connection with identified endpoints, direction, and type, such as a citation or documentary succession. Record whether it is explicit in the source or derived by a stated rule.¶

IRTF research group (RG):

A research group whose eligibility and historical identity are established in the study inventory.¶

IETF working group (WG):

A working group relevant to an observed IETF destination or process. A current affiliation is not automatically a historical affiliation.¶

Origin and destination:

The research-side and IETF-side endpoints of a proposed contribution. An endpoint can be a document or a group, depending on the inquiry.¶

Candidate:

A hypothesis selected for review. Documentary candidates concern document pairs, organizational candidates concern RG/WG processes, and human mediation candidates concern an observed person/address hint and its possible connection to a contribution.¶

Discovery signal:

A recorded reason for proposing a candidate. Several signals and paths can support the same candidate. Signals are not findings.¶

Evidence observation:

A located observation in an identified source version, with provenance, integrity information, date information, and a stated relationship to the hypothesis.¶

Review:

A person's justified decision about a candidate and its evidence. Corrections retain the preceding decision in the review history.¶

Consolidated decision:

Agreement between independent review roles, or an adjudicated decision under the study plan. This is distinct from consolidating several document pairs into one scientific event.¶

Confirmed contribution event:

A contribution meeting the applicable confirmation conditions after review. A single event can carry multiple mechanism and object labels. Related candidates need an explicit event-consolidation decision before they can be counted as one event.¶

Event consolidation:

A reviewed grouping of hypotheses concerning the same contribution, destination activity, and episode under the declared event unit. Its mapping and rationale are separate from reviewer agreement.¶

Study execution:

An investigation associated with a frozen study-plan version, historical cutoff, inventory, and reviewer assignments. Sources and derived snapshots can accumulate within that fixed scope.¶

Discovery records, reviewed decisions, and consolidated events are distinct units. Use their names and denominators explicitly in reports.¶

3. Study Design and Eligibility

Start from the research question and the proposed origin and destination universe. Establish the choices below before collecting evidence. The output is a versioned plan that another team can follow and audit.¶

3.1. Versioned Study Plan

Before collection, register the following study choices:¶

  1. The research question, inclusion and exclusion criteria, and intended interpretation of a contribution.¶

  2. The historical observation window, including an explicit upper cutoff in UTC and the precision of available event dates.¶

  3. A verified inventory of active and concluded RGs, with acronyms, persistent source identifiers where available, historical identity evidence, and a documented inventory audit.¶

  4. The publication plan for results, evidence, and limitations.¶

  5. The eligible reviewer and adjudicator pools, the policy for assigning an independent pair to each candidate, and conflict-of-interest exclusions, or explicit limitations where those roles cannot be assigned.¶

  6. Procedures for coverage auditing, independent review, disagreement resolution, and assessment of agreement before adjudication.¶

  7. The selection design if only part of the eligible universe will receive inspection or independent review, and which findings that design can support.¶

  8. The contribution-event unit, grouping rules, and intended measures, including the numerator and denominator for any rate.¶

  9. The discovery routes, source-specific screening conventions, and procedures for recording exclusions and rule changes.¶

Freeze these choices before beginning the investigation. Specify whether the documentary scope includes all recoverable history, drafts and RFCs in all states, and all recoverable IETF destinations, or a justified subset. The cutoff is an upper bound, not the beginning of the search period; record any lower bound separately.¶

Dividing acquisition or screening into operational batches does not constitute scientific sampling. If the question, eligibility scope, or scientific confirmation criteria change, register a new plan version and a separately identified study execution. Applying an updated discovery rule to preserved sources can enlarge a queue within the existing scope, provided the rule version, input snapshot, and earlier decisions remain auditable. Identify the version of this method and register any deviations or extensions.¶

3.2. Endpoint Eligibility

Establish an origin's IRTF affiliation from preserved group, document, or historical records, checked against the frozen inventory. A name prefix alone is insufficient. Establish the destination's IETF affiliation independently; publication as an RFC is insufficient. For confirmation, also identify the research output or agenda and its connection to the RG at the relevant time. Document affiliation alone does not establish that every idea in the document originated there.¶

Retain ambiguous, unknown, and multiple affiliations explicitly. An unambiguous current label does not resolve a conflicting history. Where a single documentary identity appears in both institutional phases, review the ordered phase observations rather than treating the two labels as two independent documents.¶

Known post-cutoff endpoints and intermediates are excluded from eligible historical paths. Undated material remains temporally pending where its eligibility cannot be established. Collection time does not substitute for an event date.¶

4. Sources, Preservation, and Chronology

Use the registered scope to acquire the material needed for discovery and review. The output is a preserved collection with identifiable versions, provenance, and an acquisition-gap record.¶

4.1. Source Coverage

Documentary sources include the Datatracker API [DATATRACKER-API] and RFC Editor material, including the RFC index [RFC-INDEX] and preserved document texts.¶

Table 3
Source material Research purpose
Document metadata, events, and relationships Identity, affiliation, continuity, and citation observations
RFC index and draft/RFC texts Publication metadata, references, and passages for content review
Group metadata, history, and selected charters RG/WG screening, institutional observations, and agenda clues
Posted draft submissions Dated author-address observations

Meeting minutes, BOF records, slides, mailing-list discussions, code, and development records can provide complementary evidence, as can participation histories and documented person aliases. Define which of these sources will be searched and how. Report the sources and historical periods actually examined, including gaps between planned and completed searches.¶

Completing the pagination of a query establishes completion of that query, not complete institutional history. Missing historical texts, unavailable revisions, inconsistent pagination, and failed requests remain visible acquisition gaps.¶

4.2. Preservation and Provenance

Preserve original response and file bytes separately from normalized records and derived candidates. Associate artifacts with source URLs, request parameters where applicable, collection times, source versions, and SHA-256 digests. Keep request logs and resumable checkpoints so that an interrupted acquisition can be distinguished from an empty result.¶

Derived observations retain their source references, transformation or rule identifiers, and analytical snapshot. Reanalysis preserves candidate identifiers, investigator annotations, and review history. A lead not proposed by a newer snapshot can remain as a historical candidate; report that condition rather than silently deleting it.¶

A checksum verifies equality with the preserved bytes. It does not prove that the source statement is true, historically complete, or independent of another source. Copies of the same declaration do not constitute independent corroboration.¶

4.3. Distinct Dates

Keep source-event time, publication time, observation time, and collection time separate. Preserve the precision supplied by the source, including year-only or month-only dates and unknown dates.¶

Charter revision observation, revision publication, and charter approval are distinct observations. Bind a date to the exact source version it supports. An approval recorded for another revision needs explicit evidence connecting those revisions. Missing or ambiguous bindings remain pending. Appendix B.2 gives a Datatracker-specific example of a conservative binding convention.¶

Even a supported charter approval date does not establish when the originating research became available or when its adoption occurred. Those are separate chronological questions for review.¶

5. Normalization and Typed Documentary Paths

Starting from preserved sources, establish document identities and connections before proposing candidates. The output retains both the observed connection and the screening interpretation.¶

Normalize document identifiers, explicit aliases, inverse relationship forms, and definite historical renames while retaining the original records. Keep documentary succession separate from technical evolution, series membership, publication stream, and standards status.¶

Preserve both the factual relationship direction and the projected direction of a possible research contribution. For example, a source record can point from a citing document to a cited document, while the contribution hypothesis is evaluated from cited work to citing work.¶

Use a preserved relationship vocabulary and record its mapping to the families below. Appendix B.1 describes the Datatracker and RFC-index conventions. These families are methodological screening categories; confirmation uses the separate T labels.¶

Table 4
Family Inputs Contribution projection
CIT: citation Normative, informative, or unspecified references; possible references retained separately Cited to citing work
CONT: continuity Definite replacement, draft-to-RFC succession, or historical rename; possible replacement retained separately Earlier work to successor
EVOL: technical evolution Update or obsolescence relationships Updated or obsoleted work to later work
FASE: institutional phase Explicit stream-change events or ordered affiliation observations of one identity or definite lineage IRTF phase before IETF phase

Normative and informative references serve different purposes [RFC3967]. Preserve that distinction without treating either subtype as automatic proof that a particular research object was used.¶

5.1. Admissible Paths

Let C denote a definite succession connection, B a cited-to-citing connection, E an update/obsolescence connection from earlier to later work, and M membership from an RFC to an STD or BCP series. The symbol * permits zero or more connections; + requires at least one.¶

Table 5
Family Admissible pattern
CONT C+
CIT C* B C*, containing exactly one citation
EVOL E+
CIT through a series M B, pending component and historical-membership review
FASE A same-identity event or ordered observations; alternatively, C+ with ordered endpoint affiliations

Direct means one admissible connection. Chain means two or more connections through intermediate items. A candidate with both direct and chain signals belongs to the "direct and chain" group. These labels describe structure, not confidence or confirmation.¶

Figure 2 illustrates these groups using CIT paths. A is an eligible IRTF origin, D is an eligible IETF destination, and X is an intermediate document. B and C retain their definitions above. The examples assume that the displayed paths are the recorded support for each pair.¶

Panel (c) has the same endpoints on both lines and is counted as one candidate. Topology groups are mutually exclusive at candidate level; individual discovery families and signals can overlap. Panel (d) does not establish use of A by D and cannot serve as a CIT path.¶

Possible references and replacements create direct leads only; they do not serve as transitive bridges. Two successive citations do not establish use of the earliest work. Shared ancestors, undirected connectivity, and shared series membership do not establish succession. Declare whether the search enumerates admissible paths or records representatives. For representative selection, document traversal limits, tie-breaking, and which family and topology combinations are retained. Make the selection reproducible from the preserved input. Recorded paths describe the search output; they do not establish that all historical routes have been found.¶

An RFC-to-series membership can resolve a series citation into a pending component hypothesis. Present-day membership does not establish historical composition or which component the citing document used.¶

Procedural approvals, conflict reviews, and standards-status processes supply context. They cannot serve as generic technical bridges or establish a stream transition or use on their own. Preserve those observations separately from admissible contribution paths.¶

For FASE, affiliation-derived ordering uses the earliest known IETF presence; an explicit event supports the local transition it records. Neither general connectivity nor endpoint ordering dates every link.¶

6. Candidate Discovery

Apply the declared discovery routes to the normalized source record. The output is a set of hypotheses with stable identifiers, supporting observations, rule versions, and unresolved checks. Record manual hypotheses with their own rationale.¶

The three categories below identify different units of inspection. One underlying case can generate related candidates in several categories. The categories do not compete as alternative final labels. Manual hypotheses can also be recorded, without inventing automated discovery signals.¶

6.1. Documentary Candidates

Documentary discovery proposes eligible origin/destination document pairs using CIT, CONT, EVOL, and FASE. It retains the supporting paths, relationship subtypes, factual and projected directions, source locations, and unresolved chronology or affiliation checks.¶

Review determines whether an identifiable research object was used, whether documentary continuity is established, or whether the relationship is only contextual. A citation is a discovery observation, not an automatic T2, T3, or T4 finding.¶

6.2. Organizational Candidates

Organizational discovery investigates an RG/WG pair and a possible process linking research to the creation or change of IETF activity. The following are derived rules, not additional Datatracker document relationship codes:¶

Table 6
Signal Screening observation
ORG_DOCUMENT_LINKS A documentary candidate connects an observed RG document and WG document
ORG_DOCUMENT_CONTINUITY That documentary support also carries continuity evidence
ORG_CHARTER_REFERENCE A verified preserved WG charter contains a located RG mention
ORG_TYPE_CHANGE Verified consecutive dated observations show RG then WG for the same persistent group identifier
ORG_AGENDA_ADOPTION A preserved charter contains explicit RG research attribution and a nearby WG work mandate

Document-supported group signals have chain topology. Verified charter mentions, same-identifier type changes, and agenda-adoption signals have direct topology. These structures do not establish group succession or research adoption.¶

The origin belongs to the frozen RG inventory. Supporting documentary candidates and artifacts belong to the same study execution. Declare whether charter searches cover all eligible groups independently or only pairs selected by earlier screening. A search restricted to previously selected pairs cannot establish coverage of all charters. Different-identifier group succession and BOF or other process routes require explicit search procedures if included in the study scope.¶

Agenda-adoption screening seeks an explicit attribution of research to an RG and a WG mandate concerning that work. Relevant mandates can include updating, integrating, adopting, standardizing, or adding work to protocols. Preserve the attribution and mandate passages separately and record the resulting signal on the RG/WG hypothesis. If lexical patterns or a proximity threshold select passages, declare their language coverage, parameters, and version before screening. Textual distance is a selection criterion, not evidence of a semantic link.¶

Review checks that both passages concern the same research result, that the origin predates the relevant mandate, and that process evidence supports the claimed connection. Textual proximity, a group mention, cooperation, or thematic similarity is insufficient. Adoption of work is distinct from succession of organizations.¶

6.3. Human Mediation Candidates

Human discovery proposes possible connections between participants and an identifiable contribution. The two address-based routes below use dated authorship observations. Declare the sources, matching rule, and treatment of identities, date precision, and affiliations. An exact-address screening profile is described in Appendix B.3. An address match remains an identity hint. Preserve source-specific author lists; RFC authorship requires its own evidence.¶

6.3.1. Documentary-Base Shared Authorship

HUM_SHARED_AUTHOR compares authorship observations on the exact endpoint document names of an existing documentary candidate. An address observed on both sides creates a possible chain through that address hint, linked to the documentary base. A single author list is not duplicated into two institutional phases of the same document.¶

The base can still be pending when discovery occurs. Document whether the search only checks authorship on the base endpoints or also applies temporal screening. Report its scope separately from the independent route; completing one search does not establish completion of the other.¶

6.3.2. Independent Author Intersections

HUM_AUTHOR_INTERSECTION searches dated authorship observations without requiring an existing documentary candidate. Establish an eligible IRTF-side observation before an eligible IETF-side observation under the declared temporal rule. Date precision and ambiguous affiliations can prevent ordering. Keep those exclusions distinguishable from a completed search with no match; Appendix B.3 describes one conservative profile for date-only submissions.¶

Pairs with an existing documentary candidate or a recorded direct relationship in either direction are separated from this exploratory queue. Remaining address matches create human mediation leads without a linked base. The absence of a recorded direct relationship does not exclude indirect paths or missing evidence. Indirect paths are not exhaustively searched by this screening step.¶

One lead represents one address/document-pair observation, not one verified person or event. Identity, historical group types, continuous affiliations, actual contribution, and mediation remain unverified. This route is a separate discovery action; completion of the documentary-base route does not imply completion of this search.¶

6.3.3. Linking and Confirming a Mediation Claim

A human lead can later be associated with a documentary or organizational base in the same study execution, with a recorded justification. That association does not confirm the contribution or the person. T6 confirmation requires an independently consolidated T1--T5 base, verified participation, and a located passage documenting mediation.¶

Figure 3 shows the path to T6 confirmation. An address match can generate a lead but cannot satisfy the identity or role conditions by itself.¶

The figure shows confirmation dependencies, not the order of all review activity. Uncertainty or rejection can be recorded while the base is unresolved. A confirmed mediation finding adds a mechanism to the existing event; it does not create another base event.¶

Changing the base retains history but prevents confirmation about the former base from being applied to the new association. If the base loses confirmation, dependent mediation becomes pending without deleting its reviews.¶

7. Inspection and Classification

The input is a candidate with its discovery basis and preserved sources. Inspection produces a located evidence record, a statement of the proposed contribution, and assessments against the criteria below. Independent reviewers evaluate that record under Section 8.¶

Inspection and review distinguish activities, not mandatory separate personnel. Each member of the assigned reviewer pair inspects the sources and develops an assessment independently within their own review workflow. A separate inspector is not required. A shared evidence packet can provide source passages and provenance; each reviewer remains responsible for checking the context and proposing their own mechanism and object labels. Keep those proposed labels and judgments concealed as specified in Section 8.1.¶

7.1. Evidence Inspection

  1. State the precise hypothesis: origin contribution, destination activity, and proposed connection. Read the discovery reason as a search lead rather than as a conclusion.¶

  2. Check endpoint identity, historical institutional affiliation, source revisions, and eligibility against the observation window.¶

  3. Identify what the origin contributed and locate the destination passage or process record showing continuity, use, or adoption.¶

  4. Examine the surrounding context, attribution, chronology, and alternative explanations. Record contrary evidence as well as support, including whether sources repeat the same declaration.¶

  5. For each proposed mechanism, explain which condition the evidence supports, contradicts, or leaves unresolved. Identify the object and its exact content, rather than inferring it from a title.¶

  6. Record source locations, dates and precision, evidence codes, gaps, and the rationale needed for another reviewer to reassess the claim.¶

Seek missing sources under a documented complementary-search procedure. Record its scope and stopping rule; lack of a located passage does not by itself establish absence of a contribution. A candidate that remains ambiguous proceeds to review with those limitations visible.¶

The following codes define the method's analytical taxonomy. Mechanisms, objects, evidence observations, and discovery signals retain separate fields as shown in Section 1.3.¶

7.2. Transfer Mechanisms

Table 7
Code Mechanism Minimum confirmation condition
T1 Documentary Verified IRTF-to-IETF continuity supported by history or an explicit statement, checked against content and affiliations
T2 Technical A located passage demonstrates use of an identifiable origin solution
T3 Conceptual Explicit attribution and use of an origin concept, model, or requirement
T4 Experimental or empirical An identifiable experimental result is used in a destination decision or specification
T5 Organizational or process A process record links a research agenda to creation or change of IETF activity
T6 Human-mediated Verified participation on both sides and a documented mediation role in an independently confirmed T1--T5 event

For T1, validate each claimed rename, adoption, publication-continuity, or institutional-phase path. Identify the research content carried forward and verify its continuity and institutional affiliations. WG adoption or a metadata relationship alone is insufficient. A T1 finding establishes documented continuity; it does not confer T2--T4 labels without their separate evidence of substantive use. For T2, normative dependence identifies the content actually used; a normative reference alone is insufficient. Similar terminology or a background citation is insufficient for T3. Citing an experiment without demonstrated use is insufficient for T4. Thematic proximity between groups is insufficient for T5. Authorship, chair status, or participation alone is insufficient for T6.¶

T6 is auxiliary: it describes mediation of an established event and cannot establish the underlying contribution by itself. Mechanisms are not mutually exclusive. Adding mechanism labels does not multiply the number of consolidated events.¶

8. Independent Review and Decision History

The input is the inspection record from Section 7.1, including preserved evidence, eligibility checks, and recorded contradictions or gaps. Each reviewer independently proposes mechanism and object labels as part of their own assessment. The output is two initial reviews and either a consolidated decision or an explicitly unresolved review status. Decision consolidation is separate from grouping candidates into contribution events, as described in Section 9.1.¶

8.1. Independent Assessments

Assign first and second reviews of each candidate to different people. A study may have more than two reviewers, with different pairs assigned to different candidates. The adjudicator for a candidate is a third person outside its assigned pair; a reviewer may adjudicate other candidates if the declared conflict-of-interest policy permits it.¶

Record the candidate identifier, assigned reviewers, their roles, and the allocation policy. Workload allocation may balance assessment counts and candidate categories and vary pairs, using a recorded seed when random tie-breaking is used. Such allocation is not a probability-sampling design for selecting candidates. Preserve existing assignments and submitted assessments when allocating new candidates; record any necessary reassignment and its reason without transferring one person's judgment to another. Operational allocation changes must be auditable and consistent with the registered study plan; changes to the declared review design require an explicit plan amendment.¶

Each reviewer checks the source passages, eligibility, chronology, and applicable confirmation conditions, then records a decision, mechanism and object labels, and a reasoned justification. The review identifies which observations support the decision and which gaps or contradictions remain. For T6, also check the confirmed-base and role-evidence conditions in Section 6.3.¶

Keep the initial assessments concealed from the other reviewer until both have been recorded. A shared evidence packet supports comparison; it does not replace each person's assessment of whether the passages support the claim. If a study reviews a sample, identify the sampled units and the records that received only one review. One positive review does not establish independent confirmation.¶

8.2. Decisions and Process Status

Apply the following decision meanings to the candidate actually under review:¶

Table 10
Decision Condition
Confirmed Eligibility and the applicable mechanism-specific conditions are supported by located evidence; the identifiable contribution and object labels are justified, and material contradictions have been addressed
Rejected Located evidence contradicts the proposed contribution, or an established eligibility failure excludes it; record the specific reason
Uncertain The available evidence cannot establish or reject the hypothesis, including unresolved identity, affiliation, chronology, use, or material contradictions

Assess support for each proposed mechanism separately. Confirm only the supported mechanism and object labels. Failure to establish T2 does not reject the whole candidate if the evidence supports T3 or another applicable mechanism; record unsupported proposals and their limitations separately.¶

Failure to locate sufficient evidence supports uncertainty rather than rejection by itself. Rejection concerns the stated hypothesis; it does not establish that no contribution occurred through another route. A decision of uncertain is a substantive review result, not an unfinished review.¶

Pending means that no effective consolidated decision exists. Record the reason separately: missing initial review, disagreement awaiting adjudication, or disagreement that remains unresolved. Preserve the individual decisions in each case. Disagreement is a comparison of reviews, not a fourth substantive decision.¶

8.3. Comparing and Consolidating Decisions

After both initial reviews exist, compare their decision states and their sets of mechanism and object labels. Agreement under this method requires all three to match. Matching reviews yield a consolidated confirmed, rejected, or uncertain decision. Record the agreement and its supporting review identifiers.¶

Agreement about a mechanism with disagreement about an object remains a disagreement for the consolidated candidate decision. The study may report label-level agreement separately, but does not report the candidate as independently confirmed while that disagreement remains unresolved. Assess agreement from the initial reviews before adjudication, as described in Section 10.¶

Figure 4 summarizes this sequence.¶

8.4. Adjudication

Assign adjudication to a third person after both initial reviews are available. Reviewers and adjudicators disclose involvement in the case or other conflicts of interest. The study plan declares how such conflicts are managed, including reassignment where needed, and the decision record identifies any remaining limitation.¶

The adjudicator:¶

  1. Identifies the disputed eligibility finding, evidence interpretation, decision state, or label, using both reviews and their sources.¶

  2. Reassesses those points against the same confirmation conditions and coding manual used in the initial reviews.¶

  3. Records a reasoned confirmed, rejected, or uncertain decision, including the effective label sets, evidence, and treatment of the conflicting interpretations.¶

The adjudicator can request additional sources or clarification. If new evidence materially changes the record, preserve a new evidence snapshot and have both original reviewers reconsider it separately, without coordinating their revised decisions, before completing adjudication. Retain the initial and later rounds separately; later judgments do not replace the original blinded reviews or their agreement measurements. A change to the confirmation criteria is a coding-manual revision, not an adjudicator's case-specific exception.¶

Adjudication can resolve a disagreement by concluding that the hypothesis is uncertain. If no adjudicator is available or the procedure cannot produce a reasoned consolidated decision, retain pending status with the unresolved-disagreement reason. Do not force agreement. Preserve both initial reviews and the adjudication rationale regardless of outcome.¶

8.5. Corrections and Reconsideration

Corrections append a new review linked to the preceding decision. Identify the reason, evidence snapshot, and which decisions are currently effective. An annotation does not silently rewrite a scientific judgment. New evidence, changed eligibility, or a changed mediation-base association can require reconsideration and renewed comparison or adjudication.¶

Reassess dependent T6 decisions when the underlying T1--T5 base changes or loses confirmation. Preserve all affected reviews and explain why a prior conclusion is no longer effective. A participation code or a complete evidence record does not establish a mediation role without substantive assessment of the located passage.¶

9. Contribution-Event Consolidation

Use reviewed hypotheses and the registered event unit to produce a versioned event mapping. Unresolved grouping decisions remain separate from established events.¶

9.1. Consolidating Contribution Events

Consolidating reviewer decisions establishes the effective judgment about a hypothesis. Event consolidation determines whether several hypotheses describe the same contribution. Removing duplicate records performs neither task by itself.¶

Declare the event unit and grouping rules in the study plan. A usable unit is an identifiable origin contribution continued, used, or carried into a destination activity in a documented episode. A contribution may include several related objects. Alternative levels of aggregation need an explicit definition and justification so results can be compared at a stated level.¶

Define episode boundaries in the plan: which documented adoption, use, or process decision begins the episode, what evidence marks completion or continuation, and what would distinguish a later decision in the same activity. Repeated mentions or publication of another version do not create another episode by themselves. Record the sources and any uncertainty supporting those boundaries.¶

For the unit above, group hypotheses into one event only when the evidence establishes all of the following:¶

  1. They concern the same identifiable origin contribution, rather than merely the same source document, author, or topic.¶

  2. They concern the same destination activity or a documented joint activity, rather than independent uses in separate destinations.¶

  3. Their chronology and process evidence support one contribution episode, with a recorded rationale for grouping them.¶

Apply these distinctions consistently:¶

Table 11
Situation Consolidation treatment
A draft and its RFC describe the same continuing use in the same activity One event; retain both document records as support
The same algorithm is independently used in two destination activities Separate events, unless evidence establishes one joint activity under the declared unit
Documentary and organizational hypotheses concern the same contribution and episode They may support one event with several independently justified mechanism labels
A person mediates an already confirmed event Attach T6 to that event; do not create another base event

Assign each event a stable identifier. Record the contribution, destination activity, episode dates and precision, supporting candidate and evidence identifiers, included mechanism and object labels, and the grouping rationale. A candidate can support more than one event when distinct contributions or episodes are separately evidenced. Combining records does not independently confirm a mechanism or object.¶

Have grouping decisions checked independently and resolve disagreements through the declared adjudication procedure. Preserve earlier mappings when new evidence requires merging or splitting events. Unresolved grouping must remain visible; report it separately or provide a justified range rather than a definitive deduplicated event total. Only events whose contribution and grouping decisions are established under the method enter confirmed consolidated-event counts.¶

10. Validation and Coverage Auditing

10.1. State What Is Being Assessed

Define the assessment question, unit, numerator, denominator, and historical scope before selecting an audit or review sample. Distinguish:¶

  • Acquisition coverage: which declared sources, queries, revisions, groups, and periods were retrieved, unavailable, or not examined.¶

  • Candidate precision: the proportion of proposed hypotheses that meet their applicable confirmation conditions after independent review.¶

  • Discovery omissions: eligible connections found by a separate search that were absent from the recorded discovery snapshot.¶

Completing declared requests measures acquisition progress; it does not establish complete historical coverage. Candidate review can assess proposed links but cannot by itself estimate connections that discovery missed. Report uncertain and pending cases explicitly and state how they enter any estimate rather than treating them as confirmed or rejected by default.¶

10.2. Audit Beyond the Candidate Queue

Audit the eligible origin universe independently of the discovered queue, including origins with no candidates. For a probability audit, declare the sampling frame, strata, sample sizes, random seed, inclusion probabilities, precision targets, and treatment of unavailable records. Use selection weights when the design requires them, and describe the estimator and its uncertainty.¶

Inspect selected origins using complementary searches. Record the sources, search procedures, eligible connections located, and their presence or absence in the fixed discovery snapshot. Apply the same confirmation criteria to audit findings as to other candidates. Document incomplete inspections and nonresponse. Report purposively selected difficult or illustrative cases separately from the probability sample.¶

A complementary search can also miss a connection. Describe how its reference set was constructed and why it is adequate for the stated assessment. An observed omission rate is conditional on that search and sample; it does not establish complete recall of all historical contributions. State which wider claims the design can support.¶

10.3. Assess Independent Agreement

Assess reviewer agreement before adjudication. Percentage agreement and Cohen's kappa can be reported with label prevalence, the reviewed units, missing decisions, and treatment of multiple labels. State whether agreement is measured per label, for exact label sets, or both. Report the sampling design if only a subset received two reviews, and preserve initial disagreements after adjudication or later corrections.¶

Document coding-manual revisions and distinguish pilot calibration from independent evaluation. Check procedural reproducibility, source coverage, and the reliability of substantive judgments separately. Reproducing a collection or count does not establish corpus completeness, validate a passage interpretation, or confirm a scientific conclusion.¶

11. Analysis and Reporting

11.1. Counts and Denominators

Report separately:¶

  • Eligible origin units and audited coverage gaps.¶

  • Unique candidates by category and discovery snapshot.¶

  • Discovery families and signals, allowing for overlap.¶

  • Direct-only, chain-only, direct-and-chain, and unknown-topology candidate groups, each candidate appearing once in this partition.¶

  • Individual and consolidated review states, including unresolved disagreements and missing reviews.¶

  • Confirmed consolidated events, unresolved event groupings, and their mechanism and object labels.¶

A transfer rate needs a validated denominator of eligible, consolidated origin units. State whether the numerator counts origins with at least one event or counts events, and ensure the units match the intended measure. Candidate counts and citation counts are not substitutes for that numerator. Label totals can exceed event totals because mechanisms and objects are multilabel. If only a sample was reviewed, distinguish findings for that sample from design-supported estimates for the eligible population.¶

11.2. Dates and Observed Reach

Publication-year charts and approximate destination dates describe the observed documentary material. They are not validated contribution dates or transfer-latency estimates. Identify the date basis, precision, and provenance of each reported series, including fallback approximations. Preserve intervals and unknown dates rather than assigning unsupported point dates.¶

For a time-to-event analysis, predefine the event, origin time, entry into observation, and follow-up period. Treat an origin with no event by the end of follow-up as right-censored only when event ascertainment is sufficiently supported over that period; justify that assumption and state the censoring date. Missing archives, incomplete searches, and unresolved eligibility are missing-information conditions, not automatically temporal censoring. Report them separately and explain any exclusions or sensitivity analysis. Unequal follow-up and changing coverage affect comparisons. No observation assigns an origin a fabricated event date or establishes that it will never contribute.¶

Observed reach can be described through confirmed destination documents, objects, or groups where the study has validated those units and affiliations. Do not interpret raw citation counts as technical use or research quality. Descriptive association does not establish that IRTF participation caused a destination outcome.¶

11.3. Reproducibility Package

Publish, subject to the evidence-release plan and privacy decisions:¶

  1. The frozen study plan, inventory audit, and coding-manual version, including event-unit and sampling definitions.¶

  2. Collection scope, requests, checkpoints, unavailable sources, and integrity manifests for preserved artifacts.¶

  3. Normalization and discovery-rule versions, analytical input snapshots, and source-to-candidate provenance.¶

  4. Evidence observations, review histories, adjudication rationales, effective decisions, and versioned event-consolidation mappings.¶

  5. Analysis definitions, filters, date bases, estimators, and the scripts or procedures needed to reproduce counts and tables.¶

  6. Coverage-audit and agreement results, or an explicit statement that these validation steps have not been completed.¶

Distinguish the historical input of a candidate from newer source collections and screening reports. Historical snapshot totals and current study-wide totals can differ because older candidates are retained and new evidence has been added.¶

12. Worked Example

All document names, passages, and decisions in this example are fictional. They illustrate the procedure and are not findings from a historical study. Assume that the dates and affiliations described as verified are supported by preserved sources within the study window.¶

12.1. From Source to Candidate

An eligible RG document A defines algorithm K in Section 3. A later eligible IETF draft B includes a normative reference to A. The preserved reference establishes the typed relationship B cites A. Discovery records a CIT signal for the proposed contribution A to B. The candidate record identifies both source versions, the reference location, the rule, and unresolved use checks.¶

At this point the bibliography supports a citation observation, not a confirmed T2 or an O7 assignment. Each assigned reviewer independently examines algorithm K as a proposed object and searches the body of B for evidence of its use, preserving the context and the search outcome.¶

12.2. Inspection, Review, and Adjudication

Suppose the first reviewer proposes confirmed T2 with O7 based on the reference, while the second records uncertain because the evidence packet lacks a passage establishing actual use. The proposed confirmation fails the T2 condition at that stage. The initial reviews disagree, so the consolidated candidate remains pending; the initial agreement assessment records that disagreement.¶

The adjudicator requests the full preserved revision of B. Inspection locates a passage in its Section 4 stating, in this fictional example, "Key derivation follows algorithm K in A, Section 3." Its context identifies the relevant computation and confirms the reference's attribution. This is recorded as a located-use observation with its version, location, provenance, and date information.¶

Both reviewers then reconsider the new evidence separately as required by Section 8.4. If their revised decisions both confirm T2 and O7, record the revised agreement and effective decision without replacing the initial disagreement or its measurement. If the revised decisions still differ, the third person completes a reasoned adjudication or records why the disagreement remains unresolved. Documentary succession has not been established in this example, so T1 is not added.¶

The same inspection can lead to other outcomes:¶

Table 12
Available evidence for the stated technical-use hypothesis Review implication
The use passage and its eligibility are verified, with no unresolved material contradiction T2 and O7 can be confirmed after the required independent decision process
The full revision is unavailable and actual use cannot be established or ruled out Uncertain; record the missing source and completed search
A verified passage explicitly states that B does not use K and discusses A only as a rejected alternative Reject that technical-use hypothesis; do not infer absence of every possible contribution

These outcomes depend on the evidence, not on the presence of the CIT signal. A matching author address would require a separate identity and mediation-role investigation before adding T6.¶

12.3. From Decisions to an Event

Suppose a later eligible revision of B and its eligible published RFC retain the same use of K in the same destination activity and documented episode. After the required evidence and grouping review, map their supporting candidate records to one event E1 with T2 and O7. Retain all document versions and the grouping rationale. Independently demonstrated use of K in a different destination activity is a separate event under this unit, unless evidence supports the declared joint-activity rule.¶

The report distinguishes the candidate records, initial and revised review decisions, and the consolidated event E1. It does not present the number of versions or citations as the number of contributions.¶

13. Methodological Limitations

Source acquisition and hypothesis discovery are separate procedures. Record the coverage and completion status of each discovery route, together with its input snapshot and rule version. An unfinished search, missing inputs, unavailable sources, and a completed search with zero matches are different outcomes. Revised rules require a new documented screening pass before their results can be compared with earlier findings.¶

Discovery does not establish technical, conceptual, or empirical use, verify person identity, or consolidate hypotheses into scientific events. These conclusions require the evidence, review, and consolidation procedures defined above. Probability audits and agreement assessment require an explicit design and completed observations. Causal or social-network claims require additional analytical designs and cannot be inferred from the discovery records alone.¶

Coverage is limited by mutable metadata, incomplete archives, missing group histories, unavailable revisions, ambiguous identities, restricted search universes, language-dependent screening, and undocumented influence. Declare these limits for each study and assess their possible effects on the findings. A zero candidate count is not evidence that no contribution occurred. Any conclusion that no evidence was found must identify the sources, historical scope, search procedures, and remaining gaps; it is distinct from a conclusion that no contribution occurred.¶

14. Security Considerations

This method does not change a network protocol. Its principal security concerns are the integrity of research artifacts, safe handling of untrusted source material, and protection of review records. Treat downloaded texts, API fields, and links as untrusted input. Rendering evidence should not execute embedded content, and access to collection, review, correction, and export functions should follow assigned roles.¶

Verify preserved bytes against their recorded hashes when deriving or exporting evidence. Retain acquisition and correction history to make unexpected changes detectable. A matching hash is not authentication of a historical claim. Exporting first-round reviews before both independent decisions exist can compromise the study's blinding procedure.¶

15. Privacy Considerations

Public submissions and process records can contain names, addresses, affiliations, and statements about individuals. Address matches remain identity hints until verified; they do not justify assertions about a person's influence, intent, or responsibility.¶

The study plan identifies what personal information is needed and what can be released. Publish only the identifying detail needed to support the research claim. Separate reviewer access from public release where appropriate, and document any redaction or restricted artifact together with its effect on reproducibility. Preserve contradictions and uncertainty rather than turning an automated address match into a personal attribution.¶

Appendix B. Source-Specific Screening Conventions

These conventions describe ways to operationalize the method with particular sources. Declare which profile a study uses, including any extensions, before interpreting its search results. Record exclusions and input versions. These choices do not replace confirmation criteria or constitute evidence of complete historical coverage.¶

B.1. Relationship Vocabulary

Preserve the Datatracker relationship catalog [RELATIONSHIP-CATALOG] and the RFC index [RFC-INDEX] used by the study. One mapping is:¶

Table 14
Source relationship or observation Family or purpose
refnorm, refinfo, refold, or parsed bibliographic references CIT, retaining normative/informative/unspecified subtype
refunk Possible direct CIT lead
replaces, became_rfc, or definite historical rename CONT with factual direction and succession order preserved
possibly-replaces Possible direct CONT lead; no transitive bridge
updates, obs, or normalized inverse forms EVOL from earlier work to later work
Explicit stream change or ordered affiliation observations FASE subject to identity, lineage, and temporal checks
contains or RFC-index is-also Series membership for pending citation-component resolution
downref-approval or conflrev Procedural context
tops, tois, tobcp, toexp, toinf, tohist Standards-status process context

Derived reference extraction, inverse normalization, and historical rename observations identify their derivation and source. Do not present them as additional codes in the source catalog. Documentary and group inventories can use persistent numeric Datatracker IDs; retain historical identifiers and aliases rather than relying only on current acronyms.¶

B.2. Charter Dates

Preserved Datatracker new_revision and iesg_approved events can support revision publication and charter approval respectively. Bind each event to its named revision. In a conservative screening profile, an approval recorded under a draft revision may be associated with the next numbered approved revision only when the preserved records show consecutive revisions and publication at the exact approval timestamp. Preserve the original event, revision identifiers, and binding rationale. A missing or ambiguous association remains pending; a different binding convention needs explicit source support and a declared rule.¶

This convention supports dates of charter events. It does not establish when research was first available, when adoption occurred, or whether the attribution and mandate concern the same work.¶

B.3. Address-Based Authorship Screening

An exact-address profile uses authorship in preserved posted draft submissions within the cutoff. Normalize the address domain while preserving local-part case. Do not infer aliases or person identity from similar names. Record the address's source document, revision, submission date, and historical-affiliation evidence.¶

For HUM_SHARED_AUTHOR, compare exact endpoint document names of a documentary candidate and preserve their separate author observations. A base can remain pending. Do not duplicate one author list into two institutional phases of a same-document case.¶

For an independent date-only HUM_AUTHOR_INTERSECTION profile:¶

  1. Require an unambiguous historical affiliation observed by the start of each submission day; retain the source date precision and the convention used to interpret that boundary.¶

  2. Require the origin submission to be strictly earlier than the destination submission. Exclude same-document pairs and record conflicting, unknown, or insufficiently precise dates or phases.¶

  3. Separate pairs with an existing documentary candidate or a recorded direct relationship in either direction. Record whether indirect paths were searched and the limits of that search.¶

  4. Keep one address/document-pair lead as an observation, not as a verified person or contribution event.¶

The day-boundary convention is conservative screening rather than a claim that affiliation histories are complete. Studies using other sources, identity hints, or temporal rules declare those differences and evaluate their effects on coverage. Confirmation still requires verified participation and a role-bearing passage in a confirmed base.¶
